# Malaysian Dietary Guidelines (MDG) 2020 — Comprehensive Knowledge Base

## 1. OVERVIEW AND PRINCIPLES

### 1.1 Key Principles of Healthy Eating in Malaysia
- Balance and moderation in all food choices
- Variety across all food groups
- Appropriate portion sizes tailored to individual needs
- Regular meal patterns (3 main meals + healthy snacks if needed)
- Home-cooked meals preferred over processed foods

### 1.2 Food Groups and Recommended Servings
1. **Rice, Bread, Cereals, and Tubers** (6-7 servings/day)
   - Choose whole grain options when possible
   - Control portion sizes to maintain stable blood glucose
   - Examples: nasi putih, mee, kuih-muih, ubi keledek

2. **Fruits and Vegetables** (3 servings fruits + 2 servings vegetables/day)
   - At least one serving of dark green leafy vegetables daily
   - At least one serving of orange-coloured fruit daily
   - Limit fruit juices; whole fruits preferred

3. **Meat, Fish, Eggs, and Legumes** (1-2 servings/day)
   - Choose lean meats and skinless poultry
   - Fish at least twice per week (omega-3 benefits)
   - Limit processed meats (sausages, nuggets, luncheon meat)
   - Legumes (kacang, tofu, tempeh) as healthy protein alternatives

4. **Milk and Dairy Products** (1-2 servings/day)
   - Choose low-fat or reduced-fat options
   - For lactose intolerance: soy milk fortified with calcium

5. **Fats, Oils, and Nuts** (5-8 teaspoons/day)
   - Use unsaturated oils (vegetable oil, olive oil) over saturated fats
   - Limit coconut milk and cream (santan)
   - Nuts in moderation as healthy snacks

## 2. SPECIFIC GUIDELINES FOR CHRONIC DISEASE PREVENTION

### 2.1 Diabetes Management (Type 2 Diabetes)

#### 2.1.1 Carbohydrate Management
- Distribute carbohydrate intake evenly across meals
- Choose low glycemic index (GI) foods: whole grains, legumes, most fruits
- Avoid high GI foods: white rice, white bread, sugary drinks
- One serving of carbohydrate = 1 slice bread OR ½ cup rice OR 1 medium apple

#### 2.1.2 Sugar Reduction
- Limit added sugars to less than 10% of total daily energy
- Avoid sugar-sweetened beverages: teh tarik, sirap, cordials, soft drinks
- Use natural sweeteners sparingly: honey, palm sugar (gula melaka)
- Read labels for hidden sugars in processed foods

#### 2.1.3 Fibre Intake
- Aim for 25-30g dietary fibre daily
- High-fibre foods: vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes
- Fibre helps slow glucose absorption and improves satiety

#### 2.1.4 Meal Timing
- Regular meal times to maintain stable blood glucose
- Avoid skipping meals
- If taking insulin or oral diabetes medications, coordinate meals with medication timing

### 2.2 Heart Disease Prevention

#### 2.2.1 Fat Intake Guidelines
- Total fat: 20-30% of daily energy intake
- Saturated fat: less than 10% of daily energy
- Trans fat: avoid completely
- Replace saturated fats with unsaturated fats (olive oil, canola oil, nuts)

#### 2.2.2 Sodium (Salt) Reduction
- Limit sodium to less than 2000mg/day (5g salt)
- Reduce soy sauce, fish sauce, belacan, budu usage
- Avoid processed foods high in sodium: instant noodles, canned foods, snacks
- Use herbs, spices, lemon, lime for flavour instead of salt

#### 2.2.3 Heart-Healthy Foods
- Oats, barley, whole grains
- Fatty fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel) - twice weekly
- Nuts: almonds, walnuts, cashews (unsalted)
- Fruits and vegetables rich in antioxidants

### 2.3 Weight Management

#### 2.3.1 Energy Balance
- Maintain healthy weight through energy balance
- Calorie needs vary by age, gender, activity level
- Average adult: 1500-2000 kcal/day for women, 2000-2500 kcal/day for men

#### 2.3.2 Portion Control Strategies
- Use smaller plates
- Measure servings initially
- Avoid eating directly from packages
- Fill half plate with vegetables

#### 2.3.3 Healthy Snacking
- Choose: fresh fruits, nuts, yogurt, roasted chickpeas
- Avoid: chips, cookies, fried foods, sugary drinks

## 3. MALAYSIAN FOOD CULTURE AND HEALTHY CHOICES

### 3.1 Traditional Malaysian Dishes - Healthier Alternatives

#### Nasi Lemak
- Traditional: coconut rice + fried peanuts + sambal + fried fish
- Healthier version: use less santan, add more vegetables, choose grilled fish
- Portion: limit to 1 cup rice, add cucumber and peanuts as garnish only

#### Roti Canai
- Choose whole wheat roti canai when available
- Pair with dhal instead of curry with coconut milk
- Limit to 2 pieces per serving

#### Char Kuey Teow
- High in fat and calories due to wok cooking
- Healthier: request less oil, add more bean sprouts, choose shrimp over liver
- Occasional treat, not regular meal

#### Laksa (Various Types)
- Asam laksa: lighter, more vegetables, fish-based broth - better choice
- Curry laksa: high in saturated fat from coconut milk
- Portion: add extra vegetables, limit noodles

#### Mee Goreng / Mee Siam
- Choose mee whole grain if available
- Request less oil and sauce
- Add vegetables for bulk

### 3.2 Beverages - Making Better Choices

#### Beverages to Limit or Avoid
- Teh tarik (sweet tea with condensed milk): high sugar and saturated fat
- Milo Dinosaur: high sugar
- Cordial drinks, soft drinks: high sugar, no nutritional value
- Air bandung: sweetened milk drink
- Cendol: coconut milk, palm sugar, ice - high sugar and fat

#### Healthier Alternatives
- Plain water: 8 glasses daily minimum
- Unsweetened tea or coffee (limit milk)
- Fresh fruit juices (no added sugar)
- Coconut water (natural, contains electrolytes)
- Barley water (unsweetened)

### 3.3 Malaysian Fruits - Nutritional Benefits

#### Low to Moderate GI Fruits (Better for Diabetes)
- Papaya (betik): rich in vitamin A, digestive enzymes
- Guava (jambu): high in vitamin C, fibre
- Watermelon (tembikai): hydrating, moderate GI
- Apple (epal): soluble fibre
- Orange (limau): vitamin C
- Banana (pisang): choose slightly unripe (more resistant starch)

#### Fruits to Consume in Moderation
- Mango (mangga): high GI, consume with protein/fat
- Durian: high calorie, high fat, consume small portions
- Rambutan: moderate sugar content
- Langsat: moderate sugar

#### Fruit Portion Guide
- One serving = 1 medium piece OR 1 cup cubed
- Limit to 2-3 servings per day

## 4. SPECIAL POPULATIONS AND CONDITIONS

### 4.1 Elderly Nutrition
- Maintain adequate protein intake to prevent muscle loss
- Ensure sufficient calcium and vitamin D for bone health
- Choose softer foods if dental issues present
- Small, frequent meals may be better tolerated
- Adequate fluid intake (water, soups) to prevent dehydration

### 4.2 Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Increased calorie needs: +300-500 kcal/day
- Folate-rich foods: leafy greens, legumes
- Iron sources: lean meat, spinach, fortified cereals
- Calcium: dairy, fortified soy products
- Avoid raw fish, unpasteurised dairy, excessive caffeine

### 4.3 Children and Adolescents
- Regular meals, especially breakfast
- Limit sugary foods and drinks
- Encourage fruit and vegetable consumption
- Adequate calcium for growing bones
- Physical activity alongside healthy eating

## 5. FOOD SAFETY AND HYGIENE

### 5.1 Safe Food Handling
- Wash hands before preparing and eating food
- Separate raw and cooked foods
- Cook food thoroughly
- Store food at safe temperatures
- Use clean water and ingredients

### 5.2 Food Storage
- Refrigerate perishable foods within 2 hours
- Use FIFO (First In, First Out) method
- Check expiry dates
- Store raw meat below ready-to-eat foods

### 5.3 Street Food Guidelines
- Choose vendors with clean preparation areas
- Ensure food is cooked thoroughly
- Avoid foods that have been sitting uncovered
- Drink bottled water from sealed containers

## 6. PHYSICAL ACTIVITY GUIDELINES

### 6.1 Recommended Activity Levels
- Adults: 150 minutes moderate activity per week
- Children: 60 minutes moderate to vigorous activity daily
- Include strength training 2-3 times per week

### 6.2 Types of Physical Activity
- Brisk walking (30 minutes/day)
- Cycling
- Swimming
- Traditional exercises: tai chi, qigong
- Household chores, gardening

### 6.3 Sedentary Behaviour Reduction
- Limit screen time
- Take regular breaks from sitting
- Use stairs instead of elevators when possible
- Walk or cycle for short distances
